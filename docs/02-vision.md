# MiceMax: vision

## In one sentence

MiceMax makes it effortless for people visiting Hokkaido for business events to stay an extra night, fill their free time and see things they couldn't see on their own, and it shows the city how much new money that brought in.

## The problem

- Sapporo hosts hundreds of conferences a year, but its main venue is full and can't take more until a new one opens around FY2033. Over the next several years, **the only way to earn more from business events is to earn more from each visitor.**
- Attendees come for the conference. Planning anything else comes second, so most of them fly in, attend and fly out.
- The city already pays to fix this, but by hand, for one conference a year, measured with a one-off survey. That doesn't scale.

See [01-evidence.md](01-evidence.md) for the numbers.

## What MiceMax does

Three people, three views, one conference link:

1. **Organizer.** Pastes the conference website link. MiceMax reads the program, finds the attendees' free time and builds a ready-to-share attendee page, QR code and email text in minutes. No extra work for the organizer.
2. **Attendee.** Opens the page (from the registration email, the conference site or a QR code on the badge). They see experiences that fit their actual free time, can join other attendees, and can book.
3. **City.** Sees how many attendees were reached, what they booked, how many extra nights they stayed and how much new money reached local businesses.

## Three ways attendees spend more

In order of priority:

1. **Extra nights.** For example: "Stay Saturday night at a Jozankei onsen", with one all-in price. This is the most clearly *new* money, because without the offer the attendee would have gone home. It works best if shown **before** attendees book flights, so it goes in the registration email.
2. **Free time during the event.** Short things that fit between sessions or after them: a morning run, a specialty coffee tour, a walking tour, a brewery tour, a group dinner with co-attendees.
3. **Specialist experiences.** Visits only this audience would value and couldn't easily arrange alone: a research facility, a farm, a factory, a lab tied to the conference topic.

## Who it is for

- **Attendees:** domestic Japanese attendees first, because they are about 94% of the market. International attendees are fully supported too. Repeat visitors who feel they've seen the main sights are likely open to newer, less-known experiences (Assumption). Attendees can also meet like-minded people at the same conference and build business relationships (for example, a golf outing with a potential client).
- **Organizers and PCOs (professional conference organizers):** a free, zero-effort add-on that makes their event more attractive.
- **The city, the DMO (destination marketing organization) and the Convention Bureau:** a way to grow and prove spend per attendee, a goal they already fund.
- **Local businesses:** customers who would otherwise not have come.

## What MiceMax is not

- Not a generic AI travel planner. Its edge is knowing the conference: who is coming, when they are free and what they care about.
- Not a conference app or registration system. It sits next to those.
- Not a tool for winning more conferences. That decision is made years ahead by professors and association boards, and Sapporo has no room for more conferences right now anyway.

## Where it can go

Start with Sapporo. Then the conferences already going to other Hokkaido cities (Hakodate next). Then Japan's other big conference cities. Every city with a convention bureau and a tourism budget has the same problem. See [04-long-term-plan.md](04-long-term-plan.md).

Every conference also makes the next one better: we learn what attendees at similar conferences chose and skipped, and that data is hard for anyone else to copy ([data flywheel](04-long-term-plan.md#data-flywheel)).

## What success looks like

A city official can say: *"Last year MiceMax reached X conference attendees, Y of them stayed an extra night, and local businesses earned ¥Z they would not have earned otherwise."* Every number in that sentence is measured, not estimated.
